Start with the reason

What’s behind the search for a top realtor?

People rarely look for help because they love filling out paperwork. There’s almost always a deeper why. When we’re honest about that, the rest of the plan gets simpler.

The weather?

Another Connecticut winter of ice, shoveling and heating bills — or the milder climate you’ve been talking about for years.

Closer to family?

Being a short drive from the children and grandchildren, for holidays and ordinary Tuesdays, instead of a trip that has to be planned.

The retirement you worked toward?

The next home you pictured when you put in all those years: less to maintain, and more of the day left for what you enjoy.

A house that’s asking too much?

Stairs, rooms you barely use, a yard that has become a second job. Sometimes the house no longer matches the way you live.

A steady pace

Most moves take six to twelve months. Here’s what that time is for.

There’s no prize for rushing a decision this large. We use the months to get the house, the belongings and the next place lined up, so closing day feels like a landing, not a scramble.

  1. Month 1

    A conversation, not a listing appointment

    Your reasons, your timing, your budget and where you’d like to land. Nothing to sign, and no pressure to decide that day.

  2. Months 1–2

    A home assessment

    We walk the house together and talk about what needs attention, what matters most, and what the property could reasonably sell for.

  3. Months 2–5

    Sorting, one room at a time

    What stays with you, what goes to family, what can be sold and what can be donated. We take it in pieces, so it never feels like the whole house at once.

  4. Months 3–6

    Auction and estate sale

    I personally coordinate auction or estate sale through Golden Gavel, a team I work with regularly, for furniture, collections and household goods.

  5. Months 4–7

    Cleanout and junk removal

    I line up the cleanout and hauling so what’s left is cleared, and the house is empty, clean and ready.

  6. Months 5–8

    Prep, photos and the sale

    Small repairs, a thorough clean, staging where it helps, then pricing, outreach to buyers, showings and negotiation — with the closing date built around your move.

  7. Moving week

    Unpacking at the new place

    I help you unpack and get set up, so the new home feels like yours from the first night, not like a stack of boxes.

If the timeline is suddenly short. A health change or a move into care doesn’t leave a year to plan. We can tighten these steps, and there are faster ways to sell. I’ll explain the trade-offs clearly, so your family can choose with open eyes.

An honest standard

A top realtor should be willing to tell you not to sell.

People who type “top realtor” into a search bar are often expecting a hard push to list. I think the opposite is the point. If staying in this house, with the right help, is the better financial decision, I will say so.

Before we talk about a sign in the yard, we set the options side by side. If staying is the stronger path, I’ll introduce you to people I trust, and you decide.

In-home caregivers

Help with daily routines, meals or rides, so remaining at home stays comfortable and safe.

Senior placement

When a community is the right next step, a placement advisor can help you compare places and levels of care.

Reverse lending

For some homeowners, a reverse mortgage through Reverse Lending Partners can unlock equity without selling.

I’m a real estate agent, not a lender or a care provider. I’ll make the introduction. The choice is yours.

More than a listing

The sale is one piece. I stay for the rest of it.

A Connecticut house that’s been home for decades usually comes with a second job: the belongings, the family conversations, and the move itself. That’s the work I build the plan around.

I personally coordinate outreach to the right buyers, auction or cleanout, and unpacking help at the new place. You make the decisions. I make sure they get carried out, and I keep family who live out of town in the loop.

Common questions

If you’re comparing realtors, these usually come up

  1. What should I look for in a top realtor in Connecticut?

    Look for someone who will tell you the truth, work with you directly, and help with the whole move — not only the listing. Ask whether they will still be involved in the sorting, the cleanout, the auction and the unpacking, or whether those pieces get handed off.

  2. How long does a move like this usually take?

    Most careful moves take about six to twelve months from the first conversation to the first night in the new place. Sorting and cleanout usually take the longest. If you need to move sooner, we can compress the steps.

  3. Do you handle the cleanout and auction, or only the sale?

    I personally coordinate outreach, auction or estate sale, cleanout and junk removal, and unpacking help at the new home, in addition to pricing, marketing and negotiation.

  4. What if we are not sure that selling is the right choice?

    Then we start there. We can compare staying with in-home care, talking with a placement advisor, or a reverse mortgage through Reverse Lending Partners. If selling isn’t the better decision, I’ll say so.
