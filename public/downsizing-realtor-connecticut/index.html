Start with the why

What's behind the decision?

Something I've learned over the years: the best downsizing moves start with the reason, not the house. When we're clear on why, every other choice gets easier.

The weather?

Another Connecticut winter of shoveling, ice and heating bills, or somewhere warmer for good.

Closer to family?

Being minutes from the kids and grandkids instead of hours, for the holidays and the ordinary Tuesdays.

The retirement you planned?

That next home you've worked hard for, with less upkeep and more time for what you enjoy.

Too much house?

Stairs, empty bedrooms, a yard that's become a job. Sometimes a home simply outgrows the way you live now.

More than a listing

The whole move, not just the sale

A house that's been home for 20, 30 or 60 years holds a lifetime of things, and every one of them comes with a decision. That's usually the part that feels heaviest, so that's where I start.

I walk through the house with you, help you sort what to keep, what goes to family, what can be sold and what can be donated, and then I line up the people who do the heavy lifting. You make the choices. I make sure they get done.

And when family lives out of state, I keep everyone in the loop, so no one has to fly in for every step.

How it works

A calm plan, usually 6 to 12 months

Most downsizing moves take somewhere between six months and a year from the first conversation to the first night in the new place. Here's what that time is for.

  1. Month 1

    A conversation first

    Your reasons, your timing, your budget and where you'd like to land. No paperwork, no pressure.

  2. Month 1–2

    A home assessment

    We walk through room by room and agree on what matters most, what needs attention, and what the house could sell for.

  3. Months 2–5

    Sorting help

    Keep, give to family, sell, donate. We take it one room at a time, so it never feels like the whole house at once.

  4. Months 3–6

    Auction and estate sale

    Furniture, collections and household goods can go to auction or estate sale through Golden Gavel, a team I work with regularly.

  5. Months 4–7

    Cleanout and junk removal

    What's left gets cleared out and hauled away, so the house is empty, clean and ready.

  6. Months 5–8

    Prep, staging and photos

    Small repairs, a fresh clean and staging where it helps, so the home shows the way you remember it.

  7. Months 6–11

    The sale

    Pricing, marketing, showings and negotiation, with the closing date lined up around your move, not the other way around.

  8. Moving week

    Unpacking at the new place

    I help you unpack and set up, so your new home feels like yours from the very first night.

An honest perspective

Sometimes the best decision is not to move

Here's something I tell people often: selling isn't always the best financial decision. For some families, staying in the home you love, with the right help, makes more sense than moving at all.

So before we talk about listing, we look at every option side by side. If staying put is the better path, I'll tell you, and I'll connect you with people I trust.

In-home caregivers

Help with daily routines, meals or rides, so staying home stays comfortable and safe.

Senior placement

When a community is the right next step, a placement advisor can help you compare places and levels of care.

Reverse lending

For some homeowners, a reverse mortgage through Reverse Lending Partners can unlock home equity without selling.

Common questions

Questions people usually ask

How long does downsizing usually take?

Most moves take about 6 to 12 months from the first conversation to settling in. The sorting and cleanout usually take the longest. If you need to move sooner, we can compress the steps.

Do I need to empty the house before it's listed?

No. Sorting, auction, cleanout and junk removal happen in stages, and I coordinate the people who do the heavy lifting. You won't be carrying boxes down the stairs.

My family lives out of state. Can they be involved?

Yes, and they often are. I keep everyone updated, and most documents can be signed electronically, so no one has to travel for every step.

Is there a cost to talk with you?

No. The first conversation is simply a conversation. If it turns out staying home is the better choice, that's a good outcome too.
