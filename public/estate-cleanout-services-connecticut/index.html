A work plan, not a rush

From the first walkthrough to the last box, usually six to twelve months

Cleanout is often the part that stalls everything else. We give it a place in the calendar, then build the sale and the move around it, rather than the other way around.

  1. Month 1

    A walkthrough and a conversation

    We go through the house together, talk about what’s in it, and agree on timing, budget and where you’d like to land. Nothing to sign that day.

  2. Months 1–2

    A home assessment

    What needs attention before a sale, what the property could reasonably sell for, and which rooms will take the most sorting time.

  3. Months 2–5

    Sorting, room by room

    Keep, give to family, sell, donate. We work in stages so no one has to decide the whole house in a single weekend.

  4. Months 3–6

    Auction and estate sale

    I personally coordinate auction or estate sale through Golden Gavel for furniture, collections and household goods that should be sold, not hauled away.

  5. Months 4–7

    Cleanout and junk removal

    I line up the cleanout crew and junk removal so what’s left is cleared, and the house is empty, clean and ready.

  6. Months 5–11

    Prep, the sale and the move

    Repairs, cleaning and photos, then pricing, outreach and negotiation, with the closing date set around your move — and unpacking help at the new place when you get there.

  7. When time is short. A sudden move, a health change or a house that has to be closed out quickly doesn’t leave a year. We can compress these steps. I’ll be clear about the trade-offs so your family can choose the pace that fits.

The reason under the work

A cleanout usually starts with a larger why

The boxes are the visible part. Underneath, there’s almost always a decision about how life should look next. We start there, because it shapes what to keep and where you’re going.

The weather?

Another winter of ice, shoveling and a house that’s hard to heat — or a change of climate you’ve been meaning to make.

Closer to family?

Wanting the next place to be near the children and grandchildren, not a long drive that has to be scheduled.

The retirement you planned?

The home you worked toward, with less to maintain and more of the day left for what you enjoy.

A house full of years?

Rooms of belongings, some precious, some simply leftover, and a wish not to leave that sorting to someone else later.

An honest pause

Sometimes the best decision is not to empty the house — and not to sell.

A cleanout can feel inevitable once the boxes come out. It isn’t always. For some families, staying in the home, with the right help, is the better financial decision. If that’s the case, I will tell you, and we can put the cleanout on hold.

Common questions

How estate cleanout actually works

What do estate cleanout services include?

A typical plan covers a walkthrough, room-by-room sorting, auction or estate sale for items that should be sold, donation, junk removal and a full cleanout, then preparation of the house for sale and unpacking help at the new place. I personally coordinate those steps.

Do we have to empty the house before it’s listed?

No. Sorting, auction, cleanout and junk removal happen in stages, so the home can be ready for photos and showings without your family doing the heavy lifting.

What happens to furniture and personal items?

You decide what stays with family, what can go to auction, what can be donated and what should be removed. I coordinate the people who carry out those choices, including auction through Golden Gavel.

How long does a cleanout and sale usually take?

Most plans take about six to twelve months from the first conversation to settling into the new home. When time is short, the steps can be compressed, and I’ll walk you through the trade-offs.
