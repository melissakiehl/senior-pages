A simple system

Five places for everything in the house

You don’t need a unique plan for every object. You need five clear lanes, and a pace that doesn’t ask you to empty the house in a weekend.

Keep

What comes with you

Furniture and belongings sized to the next rooms, plus the pieces that make a new place feel like home on the first night. If you can, measure the next space before you promise a sofa a second life.

Family

What someone you love would like

Ask early, and set a date to pick things up. Photograph items and share the pictures if relatives live out of state, so no one has to guess from a phone call.

Donate

What can still be useful to someone else

Usable clothing, kitchenware, books, linens and furniture in good condition. Keep the receipts in a folder for your tax records, and schedule a pickup or drop-off so boxes don’t sit in the hallway for months.

Sell

What can go to consignment, an estate sale or auction

Furniture, collections and household goods often do better in a sale than in a series of one-off listings. I personally coordinate that outreach, so you aren’t hunting down companies on your own.

Haul

What should be recycled or taken away

The leftovers after the other four lanes are done: broken items, old paint and chemicals, electronics, and the last of the clutter. Junk removal and a proper cleanout are part of the plan, not an afterthought.

Why this part feels heavy

The stuff is never only stuff

A Connecticut house that’s been home for 20, 30 or 60 years holds a life: holiday dishes, tools, the chair someone always sat in. That’s why a pile of boxes can stop a move cold, even when the reason for moving is already clear.

Maybe the reason is the weather, and another winter you’re not sure you want to manage. Maybe it’s being closer to family. Maybe it’s the next home you’ve worked toward for years. The belongings still have to go somewhere, and they deserve a process that isn’t a single exhausting weekend.

We take it room by room. You make the choices. I make sure they get done.

How the work unfolds

Usually 6 to 12 months, not one frantic week

The sale of the house sits in the middle of this timeline, not at the start. Sorting and destinations come first, so the listing is the easier part.

  1. Month 1

    A conversation

    Your reasons, your timing, and whether staying might actually be the better financial decision.

  2. Months 1–2

    Assessment

    A room-by-room walk, photos for family at a distance, and a first pass at the five lanes.

  3. Months 2–5

    Sorting

    One room at a time. Keep, family, donate, sell, haul. A few boxes a week is a real plan.

  4. Months 3–6

    Sale of belongings

    Consignment, estate sale or auction for the pieces that should be sold. I coordinate the outreach.

  5. Months 4–7

    Cleanout

    Donations go out, leftover items are hauled, and the house is cleared for photos and showings.

  6. Months 5–11

    The home sale

    Prep, pricing and a closing date built around the move, not the other way around.

  7. Moving week

    Unpacking

    The keepers arrive at the new place, and I help you unpack so the first night feels settled.

When time is short. A health change or a sudden move doesn’t leave a year to sort a lifetime of things. We can compress these steps and still give each item a destination. I’ll be honest about the trade-offs.

An honest perspective

Sometimes the kindest plan is to leave things where they are

Before we label a single box, we should ask whether a move is the right financial decision at all. For some families, staying in the home you love, with the right help, makes more sense than packing it up.

Common questions

Questions people usually ask

What should we do first with a house full of things?

Start with one lower-emotion room and sort into five groups: keep, family, donate, sell, and recycle or haul away. Photograph items so relatives who live far away can take part. Don’t try to empty the house in a weekend.

What if family members want the same items?

Name a date for people to choose and pick things up, and keep a shared list of decisions. Photos help when relatives live out of state. A calm, early conversation prevents most later surprises.

Does everything have to be gone before the house is listed?

No. Donation, consignment, estate sale or auction, and junk removal can happen in stages. I personally coordinate that work so the home is ready for photos and showings without you doing the heavy lifting.

What happens to things that don’t sell?

Unsold items can usually be donated, recycled or included in a junk-removal day after the sale or auction. That last pass is part of the cleanout plan, not an afterthought.
