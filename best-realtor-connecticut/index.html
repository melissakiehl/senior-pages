The real starting point

Before “best realtor,” it helps to name the why.

A search like this almost always begins with a change that’s already underway. Naming it makes every later choice kinder.

Is it the weather?

Another season of ice, heavy snow and a house that’s expensive to heat — or the milder place you’ve been picturing in the quieter months.

Is it family?

Wanting to be minutes, not hours, from the people you love, for the holidays and for the ordinary midweek evenings.

Is it the retirement you earned?

The next home you worked toward for years: fewer stairs, less upkeep, and more of the day left over.

Is the house simply too much?

Empty bedrooms, a yard that has become a job, rooms you close off in winter. A home can be well loved and still be more than you want to carry.

An honest measure of “best”

Sometimes the best work I do is helping a family stay.

Selling is not always the best financial decision. For some households, remaining in a familiar home — with the right help in place — is the wiser path. If that’s true for you, I will say so, and I will not talk you into a listing.

I’m a real estate agent, not a lender or a care provider. I’ll make a trusted introduction, and you decide what fits.

Common questions

Questions that come up when someone is looking for the best fit

How do I choose the best realtor for our situation?

Look for a fit, not a slogan. Ask whether you’ll work with that person directly, whether they’ll tell you if staying is wiser than selling, and whether they will still be involved in the sorting, cleanout, auction and unpacking — or only the listing.

Will I work with you, or with a team I haven’t met?

You’ll work with me directly. I personally coordinate outreach, auction or cleanout, and unpacking help at the new home. When specialists are needed, I bring them in and stay in the middle of the plan.

How long should we plan for?

Most careful moves take about six to twelve months from the first conversation to settling in. Sorting usually takes the longest. If you need to move sooner, we can compress the steps.

Is there a cost to talk it through?

No. The first conversation is simply a conversation. If it turns out that staying home is the better choice, that’s a good outcome too.
