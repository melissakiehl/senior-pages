Start with their reasons

What is really behind the move?

The most useful first step is not a listing date. It is getting clear on why a change is even on the table. When the family agrees on the why, the rest of the plan is easier to pace.

  • The winters?

    Another Connecticut season of ice, shoveling and a house that is hard to keep warm, or a place that asks less of them when the weather turns.

  • Closer to family?

    Being down the road for ordinary Tuesdays, not three hours away when something comes up, or when everyone simply wants to have dinner together.

  • The retirement they worked toward?

    The simpler home they planned for, with less upkeep and more energy left for what they still enjoy.

  • The house itself?

    Stairs, empty bedrooms, a yard that has become a second job. Sometimes a house simply no longer matches the way they live now.

What I handle

You make the choices. I keep the work moving.

A house that has been home for decades holds a lifetime of things, and every one of them comes with a decision. That is usually the part that stalls a family, so that is where I start.

I walk through the house with you and your parents, help sort what stays with the family, what can be sold and what can be donated, and then I personally coordinate the outreach, the auction or estate sale, the cleanout and the unpacking at the new place. You will not be left to find those people on your own.

If you live out of state, I keep everyone in the loop, so no one has to fly in for every appointment.

A pace that fits the family

Most of this work takes 6 to 12 months

From the first conversation to the first night in the new place, this is usually a half year to a year of steady, unhurried steps. Here is what that time is for.

  1. 1Month 1

    A family conversation

    Their reasons, your timing, the budget and where they would like to land. No paperwork and no pressure to list.

  2. 2Month 1–2

    A home assessment

    We walk through room by room, note what needs attention, and talk honestly about what the house could sell for.

  3. 3Months 2–5

    Sorting, one room at a time

    Keep, give to family, sell, donate. We never try to do the whole house in a weekend.

  4. 4Months 3–6

    Auction and estate sale

    Furniture, collections and household goods can go to auction or estate sale through Golden Gavel, a team I work with regularly. I coordinate that outreach myself.

  5. 5Months 4–7

    Cleanout and junk removal

    What is left is cleared and hauled away, so the house is empty, clean and ready. I line up the crew and stay with the process.

  6. 6Months 5–8

    Prep, staging and photos

    Small repairs, a thorough clean and staging where it helps, so the home shows the way your family remembers it.

  7. 7Months 6–11

    The sale

    Pricing, marketing, showings and negotiation, with the closing date lined up around the move, not the other way around.

  8. 8Moving week

    Unpacking at the new place

    I help with unpacking and setup, so the new home feels like theirs from the first night, not like a pile of boxes.

When time is short. A health change or a sudden move to a community does not leave a year to plan. If that is where you are, we can compress these steps. I will walk you through the trade-offs honestly, so the family can choose what actually fits.

An honest first question

Sometimes the best decision is for them to stay

I tell families this often: selling is not automatically the best financial decision. For some parents, staying in the home they love, with the right help, is wiser than a move.

So before we talk about listing, we look at the options side by side. If staying is the better path, I will say so, and I will connect you with people I trust.

In-home caregivers

Help with daily routines, meals or rides, so remaining at home stays comfortable and safe.

Senior placement

When a community is the right next step, a placement advisor can help compare places and levels of care.

Reverse lending

For some homeowners, a reverse mortgage through Reverse Lending Partners can unlock equity without selling.

I am a real estate agent, not a lender or a care provider. I will make the introduction, and your family decides what fits.

Common questions

What families usually ask first

How do we even start the conversation with our parents?

Start with their reasons, not a listing date. The first talk can cover timing, the house itself, and whether staying with some help might actually be a better fit. There is no obligation to sell, and I am happy to sit in on that conversation if it would help.

We live out of state. Can you still help with our parents' home?

Yes, and I often do. I keep the whole family updated, and most documents can be signed electronically, so no one has to travel for every step.

Do we have to empty the house before it is listed?

No. Sorting, auction, cleanout and junk removal happen in stages, and I personally coordinate the people who do the heavy lifting.

What if our parents would rather stay in the house?

That may be the right call. If staying looks like the better path, I will tell you, and I can introduce you to in-home caregivers, a senior placement advisor, or Reverse Lending Partners.

Is there a cost to talk with you?

No. The first conversation is simply a conversation. If it turns out a move is not the best step, that is a good outcome too.
